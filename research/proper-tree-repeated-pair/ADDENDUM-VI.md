# Reconstruction when repeated weights form an independent set

**Theorem.** For a proper tree whose skeleton vertices of repeated weight form an independent set, the specialized first power-sum derivative determines the tree among proper trees. There is no edge-sum restriction. In fact the overlap matrix of Addendum V always has full column rank for this class.

The reconstruction now follows from Addendum V, including the use of the weight multiset and edge counts from Awan's Lemma 2.4. Our rank argument adapts the leading-term and Laurent-monomial methods of Awan's Lemmas 2.2–2.3 to differences of triple and disjoint-edge templates. If there are no columns the assertion is vacuous. Every column is indexed by a repeated center weight a and two distinct unique neighbor weights u,v; in particular a,u,v are pairwise distinct and at least 2. Write C for their multiset and s for their sum. All expressions below use unsigned templates, as in Addendum V.

## Leading monomials

Order parent partitions lexicographically with parts in descending order. The parent is the partition of component weight sums before marking. Its parts are all at least 2. For each nonzero template, its leading marked monomial is obtained by decrementing a smallest part at least 3 and resorting; call this map f on nonzero parents. In fact all such parts have positive marking coefficient: component weight s is at least twice component size c, hence s-c>0, and s≥3 makes the resulting index at least 2. Multiple markings making the same monomial only add positive coefficients. Decrementing an available smaller part leaves at least as many of every larger part and so is lexicographically preferable.

For parents P>Q of the same sum and length with nonzero templates, f(P)≥f(Q). Moreover equality is possible only as follows: after stripping the same prefix, the two respective residual lists are (t,2,...,2) and (t-1,3,2,...,2) for some t≥4. Here is the comparison. Let t be the largest weight where multiplicities differ. Equal sum and length imply t≥3, and P has more copies of t. Therefore the decrement in P cannot occur above t. The first difference survives decrementing unless P decrements t itself and had exactly one extra copy; any decrement of Q above t only makes Q lexicographically smaller at a higher index. In the exceptional case P has no part strictly between 2 and t, since t was its smallest markable part. Strip all common entries at and above t. P's residual list is one t followed by 2s; Q's residual parts are below t. By the equal sum and length, if the largest residual part of Q is t-1 it leaves exactly one unit above the all-2 baseline for the remaining parts, hence one 3 and otherwise 2s. Decrementing the 3 in Q produces exactly f(P) (when t=4, decrement either 3). In all other cases Q's residual largest part is at most t-2, and f(P)>f(Q). This proves the asserted comparison and collision form.

The triple parent for C is the multiset W - C + {s}; its paired disjoint parent is W - {a,a,u,v} + {a+u,a+v}. The triple parent is larger: compared with the disjoint parent its largest difference is the added part s. The disjoint parent cannot have gained s-1 instead, since a+u,a+v≤s-2 and the change in singleton parts is only at a. Thus the two parents cannot collide under f. The leading monomial of the overlap column is accordingly f(triple parent). Distinct columns have distinct triple parents: any difference of triple totals is detected at the larger total, since a triple's three weights are each smaller than its own total. With equal totals the parent determines the deleted triple, whose only repeated color in W identifies its center.

## Ruling out a kernel

Suppose there is a nonzero rational relation among columns. Restrict to its nonzero coefficients, let S be the largest triple total there, and let M be the largest weight of W. For this part only, divide all polynomials by the common monomial P_W, the product of one p_w for each occurrence of w in W, and work with Laurent monomials. Unmarked triple and disjoint templates divided by P_W have ratios
```
 p_s / (p_a p_u p_v),
 p_(a+u) p_(a+v) / (p_a^2 p_u p_v).
```

**If S<M**, the coefficient of p_M to power -1 can arise only by marking an untouched singleton of weight M. All nonsingleton component sums are at most S. The coefficient is thus the same nonzero factor (M-1) times the multiplicity of M times p_(M-1), multiplied by the corresponding relation of unmarked differences. In that unmarked relation the monomials with exponent +1 at p_S come only from triples of total S; their three denominator colors distinguish them. This contradicts a nonzero coefficient at total S.

**If S≥M**, the Laurent monomials with exponent +1 at p_S arise only from triples of total S with a singleton marked. No singleton can be marked down from S+1, every disjoint nonsingleton part is at most S-2, and a marked triple has index at most S-1. Fix any center a≥3 of a total-S column. Marking a remaining singleton of weight a contributes a monomial of exponent -2 in p_a and exponent +1 in p_S. There is at least one such singleton because a is repeated. No other center can remove a as a triple neighbor (neighbor colors are unique), and a singleton marking from any other weight cannot account for the second denominator p_a. For the same center a and total S, this monomial distinguishes the neighbor pair after canceling the common factor p_S p_(a-1)/p_a^2. Its nonzero coefficient therefore forces that column's coefficient in the relation to be zero. Hence every still-active total-S column has center 2.

Let P now be the largest triple parent in the relation. It has total S, because increasing the triple total increases the parent lexicographically. Its center is 2. Its column's leading monomial cannot cancel unless another triple parent Q<P has the same f-value. The collision form requires P to contain exactly one more 2 than Q. But a triple parent removes at most one 2 from W (its three colors are distinct), and the triple of P, centered at 2, already removes one. No triple parent can contain fewer 2s than P. This rules out the tie.

There is therefore a unique largest leading monomial in the supposed relation, a contradiction. The overlap columns are independent, so the criterion of Addendum V proves the theorem. The argument is symbolic and imposes no scan or bound on the size of the skeleton.

## Comparison

[Awan's Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13464v2#S2) assumes repeated weights occur only at skeleton leaves. In skeletons on at least three vertices such leaves are nonadjacent. The present criterion also allows repeated weights at internal vertices as long as no edge joins two vertices of repeated weight; the 47-vertex example in Addendum V shows strict inclusion. This is a result for a class of proper trees, not for all proper trees.
