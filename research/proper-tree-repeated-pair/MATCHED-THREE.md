# Three forest layers for proper trees with matched repeated weights

**Theorem.** Let a proper tree \(T\) have weighted skeleton \(K\) with weight multiset \(W\). Assume that distinct nonempty submultisets of \(W\) of cardinality at most four have different sums. For each weight \(a\) that occurs at least twice, form the bipartite graph between the vertices of weight \(a\) and the uniquely weighted vertices, retaining their cross edges. If each such graph has a matching saturating the weight-\(a\) side, the full chromatic symmetric function \(X_T\) determines \(T\) among trees. After recovering \(W\) and properness, only the first three weighted forest layers are needed. The condition is equivalent to the corresponding incidence block \(N_a\) having full column rank over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

This weakens the **structural** requirement of type-private representatives: a unique vertex may be adjacent to several vertices of one repeated weight, and a repeated vertex need not have any private neighbor. The proof also works under query-specific isolation: only the complete profiles formed by appending singleton complements to the connected triples and two- and three-pair templates queried below have to be isolated. An infeasible template is counted as zero without an isolation test.

## Decoding weighted forest counts

A proper tree has at least one leaf attached to every internal vertex. Keep its internal vertices as the skeleton, with weight \(w(v)=1+\#\{\text{leaves adjacent to }v\}\). The weight is at least two. The tree is recovered from the skeleton by reattaching \(w(v)-1\) leaves. Trivial trees of order at most two are handled by their order.

By [Orellana–Tomba, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18940v1#S3), Theorem 3.10 and Remark 3.11 recover \(W\) from the leading star-basis partition, Corollary 3.13 forces any tree with equal \(X_T\) to be proper, and Proposition 3.8 gives the weighted forest coefficients: in the part with no \(1\)'s only the zero-isolate term per component remains, with coefficient one (a component on \(k\) skeleton vertices has total weight at least \(2k\)). The coefficient of a \(1\)-free partition thus counts edge subsets of \(K\) whose components have those total weights. A partition with \(|W|-r\) parts comes from \(r\) selected edges. For \(r\le3\) every component has at most four vertices. By the arithmetic assumption each component sum gives its underlying weight multiset. Specify desired nonsingleton blocks and append the singleton complement to query that coefficient. If their combined weights exceed the available multiset, set the count to zero. More generally, a feasible complete profile is isolated when no different block profile with the same underlying multiset and number of blocks has the same multiset of block sums; such a profile reads directly from its coefficient without assuming global separation. The one-edge coefficients identify edge types without an arithmetic hypothesis: divided by the all-singleton formal monomial, an edge of weights \(c,d\) gives \(p_{c+d}/(p_c p_d)\), whose positive index exceeds each negative index. A connected triple corresponds to one edge subset, because an induced connected subgraph of a tree is a tree. A profile consisting of two or three pair blocks counts unordered disjoint edge sets.

## Recovering unique-to-repeated incidence

Let \(U\) be the set of uniquely weighted vertices, indexed by their weights. Let \(R_a\) denote the vertices of repeated weight \(a\). Write \(N_a[u,i]=1\) for \(u\in U,i\in R_a\) adjacent. The one-edge layer determines edges inside \(U\) and each row sum of \(N_a\). Distinct \(u,v\in U\) share a neighbor in \(R_a\) precisely when \(uv\) is absent and the connected triple with weights \(\{w(u),w(v),a\}\) has positive count. If \(uv\) is present acyclicity excludes a shared neighbor. Otherwise a four-cycle excludes more than one shared neighbor.

Construct the graph on \(U\) in which pairs with such a neighbor are adjacent. The neighborhood in \(U\) of each \(i\in R_a\) with at least two such neighbors is exactly one maximal clique of size at least two. Indeed, three vertices in a triangle in this graph must share one witness in \(R_a\): if two witnesses coincide the third must also coincide (no four-cycle); if they are all distinct they give a six-cycle. In a larger clique fix any pair; its witness is unique and is also the witness for each third vertex. Thus the maximal cliques recover all columns of support size at least two. Subtract their occurrences from each row sum to recover singleton columns. The known multiplicity \(|R_a|\) then gives the number of empty columns. This recovers each \(N_a\) up to arbitrary column ordering, uniformly in any competitor with the same \(X_T\). The matching condition is detectable from these recovered matrices. A nonzero full-column minor implies a saturating matching by the determinant expansion. Conversely a saturating matching chooses a square submatrix with a perfect matching, necessarily unique because the incidence graph is a forest. The corresponding determinant is \(\pm1\).

## Recovering repeated-to-repeated adjacency

Fix repeated classes \(a,b\), possibly equal. Let \(E\) be the number of edges of type \(\{a,b\}\), counted once each, and \(A_{ab}\) the matrix with rows \(R_a\), columns \(R_b\), whose entries indicate adjacency. For \(a=b\), use the symmetric zero-diagonal adjacency matrix. Write \(d_a(i)\) for the number of edges of the target type incident to \(i\in R_a\) and define \(d_b\) analogously.

Let \(D(u;a,b)\) be the two-pair profile count of edges \(\{w(u),a\}\) and \(\{a,b\}\). The middle edge type never touches \(u\). For every edge \(ui\) to \(R_a\), exactly \(E-d_a(i)\) middle edges avoid \(ui\). Since the two types are distinct, each counted pair is counted once. Thus the coordinate \(u\) of \(N_a d_a\) is
\[
E\sum_i N_a[u,i]-D(u;a,b).
\]
This gives \(d_a\) by any left inverse \(L_a\) of \(N_a\); similarly recover \(d_b\). No doubling enters when \(a=b\).

For distinct \(u,v\in U\), let \(\Omega_{uv}\) consist of \((i,j)\in R_a\times R_b\) such that \(ui,vj\) are both edges and \(i\ne j\). Put \(P=|\Omega_{uv}|\), and let \(M\) be the three-pair profile count for edge types \(\{w(u),a\}\), \(\{w(v),b\}\), \(\{a,b\}\). All types differ, even for \(a=b\). Given an outer pair \((i,j)\), the number of middle edges disjoint from both outer edges is \(E-d_a(i)-d_b(j)+A_{ab}[i,j]\), by inclusion–exclusion. Terms with \(i=j\) when \(a=b\) must be omitted from the degree correction; they contribute zero to the adjacency matrix product. Summing gives
\[
(N_a A_{ab} N_b^{\mathsf T})[u,v]
=M-EP+\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega_{uv}}(d_a(i)+d_b(j)).
\]
The right-hand side is known. A diagonal entry \(u=v\) is zero: a nonzero term would require the triangle on \(u,i,j\), with \(i=j\) excluded by looplessness. Therefore if \(H\) is the recovered product, \(L_aH L_b^{\mathsf T}=A_{ab}\). All edges in the skeleton are recovered up to consistent relabeling within repeated classes. The same reconstruction in any tree with equal \(X_T\) proves the assertion.

If \(E=0\), the corresponding block is already zero. In particular the inversion proof only needs full rank for classes incident to at least one repeated-to-repeated edge, a condition also detectable from the one-edge layer and the recovered incidence matrices.

## Exact witnesses

Use weights \((2,2,2,2,5,5,13,24,46,87,167,310)\) at vertices \(0,\ldots,11\), total 665. Common skeleton edges are \(06,16,07,27,18,39,4\!-\!10,5\!-\!11,13\), with \(13\) meaning \(1\!-\!3\). Add \(04,35\) to the first skeleton and \(05,34\) to the second. Vertex \(0\)'s only unique neighbors are \(6,7\), each incident to two vertices of weight two. A saturating matching for that class is \(0\mapsto6,1\mapsto8,2\mapsto7,3\mapsto9\), and for weight five it is \(4\mapsto10,5\mapsto11\). Direct enumeration gives 230 distinct short sums, identical weighted forest layers 0–2, and counts \(2\) and \(1\) at the layer-three profile \((2,2,7,15,24,46,87,172,310)\). This pair witnesses that two layers do not suffice on the stated class, not a collision for the full invariant. The [exact checker](checks/matched_three.mjs) recovers all four ordered repeated-class blocks in both examples from the layer tables, including the within-class weight-two edge.

A second version replaces the final weight 310 by 313, for a total of 668 vertices. Global short-sum separation now fails because \(313=13+46+87+167\). Nevertheless, [exact enumeration](checks/matched_arithmetic.py) verifies isolation for all 48 two-edge and 45 three-edge profiles in a superset of the required queries (including both orders of distinct repeated classes). Running the matched checker with `--isolated-fixture` verifies the same layer-0–2 coincidence and all adjacency blocks, with third-layer counts 2 and 1 after substituting 313 in the displayed profile. Thus the arithmetic relaxation genuinely enlarges the matched class.

For context, see the marked two-edge antecedent in [Aliste-Prieto, de Mier, Orellana and Zamora](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.11787), Lemma 7.4(b), and their unmarked overlap analysis in the proof of Theorem 7.13. The theorem concerns the full invariant and makes no claim about a specialized derivative, all trees, or priority.
